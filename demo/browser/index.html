Five Letters, 270M Parameters

A tiny language model, Gemma 3 270M, trained on free GPUs to play the five-letter word game through text. It reads the feedback, writes short notes on what it knows, and guesses one word per turn.

The browser runs the final model alone with greedy decoding, as in the paper. On the 100 WordleBench words, the 8-bit copy played 83 games exactly like the paper's model and won 83 (paper: 87); the full-precision copy reproduced all 100 games exactly (won 87, as in the paper). Word-level decoding needs the full code; its games are in the recorded tab.

Every recorded game

The exact games behind the paper's numbers, replayed from the records. Test sets were scored once, for a model declared in advance; the development sets chose that model.

How it was trained

  1. Teacher games. The model imitates a solver that only plays words consistent with every clue.
  2. Spelling drill. It then followed the clues but could not recall real words that fit them, so it practised writing real words for thousands of boards.
  3. Recovery round. When the game rejected a non-word, it wrote the same word again. One round of training on boards with its own rejected words fixed most of these repeats.
